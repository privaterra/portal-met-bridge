**portal-met-bridge 0.18.0 — alpha.** Meticulous & Belka Portal conductivity data visualization & analysis: a local web app that records a [Belka Portal](https://www.belka.coffee/)'s conductivity stream alongside a [Meticulous](https://meticuloushome.com/) espresso shot, integrates it against the cup weight, estimates TDS from your own refractometer readings, reviews shots with an AI model of your choice, hands the brew to Beanconqueror, and shows a machine dashboard. Runs on your Mac (or a Raspberry Pi) and talks to the Portal and the machine from Chrome; nothing leaves your network except the AI review you ask for.

Third alpha. Everything in [0.17.1](https://github.com/privaterra/portal-met-bridge/releases/tag/v0.17.1) still holds — the Portal driver follows the published protocol but has not yet been proven on the author's own Portal — plus the changes below.

## What changed since 0.17.1

- **Beans tab** (between Analysis and Machine): every coffee you have brewed or entered, with roaster, origin, process, roast level, how many shots, its average rating, when it was last brewed, the last dose and grind, and a link. Click a coffee for its shots in History, *Use* to put it in *This coffee*, *Edit* for its details, notes and the **web page** where its information lives (shown as *info ↗* beside the Bean field) — the edit can rewrite the coffee's shots too, so a corrected name or origin follows through. *Add a coffee* before its first shot.
- **Rating settings** (Settings → Recording → Rating): ratings on or off; a **1–5 or 1–9** star scale (each shot keeps the scale it was rated on — History shows *★7/9* beside *★4* and sorts by the fraction); and **when to rate**: manually afterwards (now the default — click the stars under the shot's title or the ★ cell in History) or when the shot completes, when the app asks.
- **Where the buttons send their files** (Settings → Files and folders): the buttons under *Files for this shot* and the History exports each either download through the browser (the default) or go to the chosen folder. *Export settings* and *Back up everything* always download; **Write a backup now asks** whether to write to the backups folder or download; **Restore… asks** whether to upload a backup file or **pick one of the backups saved** in the backups folder (the automatic `portal-met-bridge-latest.json` and the dated copies — also on the Raspberry Pi's `/share`).
- **History → Export selected**: a zip of just the ticked shots (samples CSV, shot JSON, graphs, PDF report, summary CSV).
- The duplicate **Shots** block on the left of the Shots tab is gone: ◀ ▶ now sit beside the shot's title, the demo buttons under Settings → Recording → Demo data, and History is the list.

## Install

**macOS — the app (no terminal).** Download `portal-met-bridge-0.18.0-macos.zip` (or the `.dmg`), move *Portal Met Bridge.app* to Applications and open it. It runs on Apple silicon and Intel (both Node runtimes are inside), starts its own small server on `http://localhost:8787`, keeps your files in `~/Documents/portal-met-bridge`, and opens Chrome. This alpha is **not signed or notarized**, so macOS stops it the first time: right-click (control-click) the app → **Open** → **Open**, or System Settings → Privacy & Security → **Open Anyway**. Once is enough. Chrome or Edge is required for the Portal's Bluetooth link. Upgrading from an earlier app: replace it; your shots live in Chrome's database and your files in `~/Documents/portal-met-bridge`, both untouched.

**Home Assistant on a Raspberry Pi 5 (or any Home Assistant machine).** Settings → Apps → *Install app* → ⋮ → *Repositories* → add `https://github.com/privaterra/portal-met-bridge` → install **Portal Met Bridge** → Start (or press **Update** on an installed 0.16 or 0.17). The page is at `http://homeassistant.local:8787`, Bluetooth needs the https address `https://homeassistant.local:8788` with its certificate trusted once (the http page links to the certificate and the steps), and the files go to `/share/portal-met-bridge`. Details: [docs/PACKAGING.md](https://github.com/privaterra/portal-met-bridge/blob/v0.18.0/docs/PACKAGING.md).

**From source.**

```sh
git clone https://github.com/privaterra/portal-met-bridge.git
cd portal-met-bridge
./start.command        # macOS: serves http://localhost:8787 and opens Chrome; or: node serve.mjs
```

**You need**: Chrome or Edge (Web Bluetooth); a Meticulous on your network (its API is on port 80 — enter the machine's IP or `meticulous.local` under Settings → Machine); the Belka app on your phone closed (the Portal takes one connection at a time); a refractometer only if you want TDS/EY estimates; an API key from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google only if you want AI reviews (the README says how to get one).

## Known limits (please read)

- **The Portal driver is untested on a physical Portal by the author.** It is written from the [variegated-rs](https://github.com/variegated-coffee/variegated-rs) driver and the [Decenza](https://github.com/janhcla/Decenza) spec and verified against a simulated Portal only. The **Connection check** under the Connect button shows each step so a failure says where.
- **EC is not TDS.** The Portal measures conductivity; TDS/EY are estimates from your own refractometer readings and shift between coffees, waters and mountings. Treat *mean EC per gram* as the primary number.
- **The AI review reads numbers.** It cannot taste the cup — that is what your rating and comment are for — or see the puck; check any figure it quotes against the *Stages, from the recording* table, and test its suggestions one at a time.
- **Unsigned macOS app; Home Assistant builds on the device** (a minute or two on a Pi 5).
- Tested against Meticulous firmware 0.2.24 (beta channel); other versions may name stages or fields differently. Web Bluetooth has no background mode: keep the tab open while pulling shots.
- The code was **written by an AI assistant from the author's descriptions** (see *How this was built* in the README) and has had one pair of eyes on it.

## What would help most

Open an [issue](https://github.com/privaterra/portal-met-bridge/issues) with a session against a real Portal (Settings → Logging and debug → log on, with packets, then the `.log` and the Connection check's steps), refractometer readings next to the app's ∫EC dm for a few shots of one coffee, or anything the machine's API answered differently.
